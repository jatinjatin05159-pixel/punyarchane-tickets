A language of line, rhythm and expression

Taught in the Pandanallur bani, as Guru Archana Punyesh received it. Every piece rests on araimandi, the half-seated stance, and moves between pure rhythm and storytelling carried by the eyes, face and hands.

The academy

A school built around one belief

That classical dance is not only something to perform, but a tradition to learn, to live with, and to pass on.

Jatin Academy of Dance began in 2004 in a room in Rajajinagar, with a handful of students and a guru who had been dancing since the age of five. Vidushi Smt. Archana Punyesh had by then trained under three eminent gurus, performed across India and abroad, and wanted a place where that same training could be handed down intact rather than diluted.

Two decades later the academy teaches more than two hundred students across four branches in Bengaluru, and over a thousand have passed through its classes. Sixteen have completed their Rangapravesham under her guidance, and many more their Gejje Pooje.

What has not changed is how a class runs. Students learn the adavus and the technique, but equally the expression, the meaning and the cultural weight of what they perform. Each student is taught at their own pace, with attention to their growth, their confidence and their presence on stage — so that they become dancers, rather than people who have learned a choreography.

Founder & principal guru

Vidushi Smt. Archana Punyesh

Natya Mayuri. Doordarshan Grade A artist in both Kuchipudi and Bharatanatyam. Forty years on stage, thirty years teaching.

She began learning at the age of five and trained under Smt. Sunanda Devi and Smt. Dharani Kashyap in Kuchipudi, and under Dr. Suparna Venkatesh in Bharatanatyam, having first studied under Vidwan Paramesh of Kanakapura. She performed her Kuchipudi Rangapravesham at Ravindra Kalakshetra in August 1996 and her Bharatanatyam Rangapravesha at ADA Rangamandira in February 2006, and completed her Vidwath in 2004–05.

Her Ardhanareeshwara in Kuchipudi is the work she is best known for, and her portrayal in Guru Smt. Dharani Kashyap’s ballet Mandodari Kalyanam is among the roles that established her. She has danced in the productions of leading choreographers in India and abroad, and teaches both forms herself at the academy.

Training

How students progress

Classes are grouped by level rather than by age, so a beginner of nine and a beginner of thirty learn the same foundation, properly.

  • Starting ageFrom four and a half years. Each level takes roughly two years, though it depends entirely on the student.
  • Teens & adultsWelcome, and taught within the regular batches rather than separately.
  • ExaminationsStudents are prepared for the Karnataka board dance examinations.
  • Online classesOne-to-one online training, for students outside Bengaluru and for those who cannot reach a branch.
  • WorkshopsIntensive workshops with visiting masters, including a three-day Varnam workshop with Dr. Sathyanarayana Raju.
  • MilestonesGejje Pooje and, in time, Rangapravesham, guided from choreography through to the stage.

The debut

Rangapravesham

The solo debut a student gives after years of training — a full evening of dance, before an audience, on her own.

  1. Six to eight years of trainingOnly once the technique, stamina and expression are genuinely ready.
  2. Six months of preparationDedicated rehearsal for the full repertoire, built around the student.
  3. The academy handles the restChoreography, orchestra, venue, costume, make-up, invitations and photography.
